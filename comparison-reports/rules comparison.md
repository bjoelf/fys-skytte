# Comparison of SRA/TTS, Viking Brutality, and Brutality Match Rules

## Executive summary

The Viking Brutality rulebook is **highly similar to SRA in its safety model**, but only **moderately similar as a complete sporting rulebook**. It uses the same conventional practical-shooting framework: a cold range, designated safety areas, unloaded transport, strict muzzle and trigger-finger discipline, immediate disqualification for unsafe gun handling, and closely matching definitions of an accidental discharge. Its scoring, divisions, administration, and procedural coverage are much simpler and materially different from SRA.

The current Brutality Match Rules are **moderately similar to SRA in safety subject matter**, but less similar in how several important risks are regulated. The most significant departure is Brutality's firearm-state model: an RO-verified-clear or Condition 3 firearm is treated as a "stick" and is exempt from muzzle, 180-degree, and dropped-firearm disqualification rules. SRA generally applies its handling sectors and unsafe-handling rules to a firearm regardless of whether it is loaded. Current Brutality is also a narrower event ruleset with Time Plus scoring, rather than SRA's broader sport-governance system and point-based scoring methods.

| Comparison with SRA/TTS | Safety similarity | Overall regulatory similarity | Overall breadth relative to SRA |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Viking Brutality | **High (4/5)** | **Moderate (3/5)** | **Much narrower** |
| Current Brutality Match Rules | **Moderate (3/5)** | **Low to moderate (2/5)** | **Much narrower** |

These ratings are qualitative assessments of rules and effects, not text-similarity percentages.

## Sources and method

This comparison uses the three supplied documents:

- *SRA-swe.txt*: Swedish SRA/TTS rulebook, version 8.0.
- *viking-brutality rulebook.txt*: Viking Brutality Official Rulebook.
- *BRUTALITY MATCH RULES.txt*: Brutality Match Rules, updated 2026-01-03.

Rules were compared by concept and practical effect in two areas:

1. **Safety:** range condition, firearm handling, transport, muzzle and finger rules, accidental discharges, abandonment, protective equipment, ammunition, steel-target distance, and sanctions.
2. **Breadth:** match administration, stage design, commands, scoring, targets, divisions, equipment, results, reshoots, and protests.

The supplied SRA document is a Swedish adoption with expressly marked Swedish and military additions. It is therefore a useful proxy for Finnish SRA, but this analysis cannot reliably attribute every SRA provision to the Finnish original rather than the Swedish adaptation. Rule references below refer to the supplied documents.

## Focus area 1: safety

### Viking Brutality compared with SRA

Viking's safety system is close to SRA in both structure and detail.

| Safety subject | SRA/TTS | Viking Brutality | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold range | Firearms outside a stage must be unloaded; loading and handling occur under RO direction (3.3.1, 3.3.8). | Cold range; firearms unloaded except under immediate official supervision (2.1-2.1.1). | **Very close** |
| Safety areas | Marked handling area; no live/dummy ammunition, loaded magazines, or ammunition handling (3.3.5-3.3.6). | Marked safety area; no firearm loading or handling of ammunition, snap caps, or feeding devices (2.2-2.2.3). | **Very close** |
| Transport | Handguns holstered/cased; long guns cased or carried muzzle up/down and unloaded (3.3.1-3.3.3). | Handguns cased/holstered, decocked, magazine removed; rifles cased or carried muzzle up/down, empty and flagged (2.3). | **Very close**; Viking makes chamber flags mandatory. |
| Accidental discharge | Includes shots outside limits, into the ground within 3 m, or evidently unintentional (3.3.17-3.3.19). | Includes shots over a berm, into the ground within 10 ft, during loading/reloading/malfunction handling, transfer, or movement (2.4.1). | **Very close**; 10 ft is approximately 3.05 m. Viking enumerates more situations. |
| Muzzle sectors and self-sweeping | Defined 180-degree sectors; muzzle may not point at the competitor (3.3.10-3.3.11, 3.4). | Rearward 180 violation and self-sweeping are DQ offences whether the firearm is loaded or not (2.4.2.2, 2.4.2.4). | **Very close** |
| Dropped firearm | Only an RO may retrieve a firearm dropped outside a stage; loss of control during a stage is tightly controlled (3.3.4, 3.3.14). | Dropping during the course of fire is a DQ; only an official may retrieve it (2.4.2.3, 2.4.2.13). | **Close**, with Viking stated more categorically. |
| Trigger finger | Finger out during draw, holstering, reloads, malfunction handling, and movement unless engaging/aiming (3.3.12-3.3.13). | Nearly the same specific prohibitions (2.4.2.7-2.4.2.9). | **Very close** |
| Loaded holstering | Permitted only in specified safe conditions; the stage may not force it (3.3.9). | Permitted only with the applicable safety/hammer condition (2.4.2.10). | **Close** |
| Abandonment | Controlled placement is allowed only under stage rules, secured and on a stable surface (3.3.14). | Allowed only as briefed, with safety applied or completely unloaded and muzzle in the designated direction (2.4.4). | **Close** |
| Steel distances | 8 m for pistol/shot; 20 m for rifle/slug, but 50 m under the Swedish military provision (6.2.6). | 8 m for handgun and 50 m for rifle (2.4.2.15). | **Close to the Swedish military variant**, more restrictive than general SRA for rifles. |
| Prohibited ammunition | Prohibited ammunition is a safety violation; detailed ammunition controls also apply (3.3.20, 5.1.10-5.1.11). | Similar ban on tracer, incendiary, explosive, AP, and penetrator ammunition (2.4.3, 3.3). | **Close** |
| Safety adjudication | RO acts; RM verifies DQ grounds (3.2.3). | Official stops the shooter; RM approves DQs and reshoots (1.7, 2.4). | **Close** |

Important differences remain:

- **Protective equipment:** Viking requires eye protection throughout the event site and ear protection on or near a stage (1.1-1.2). SRA expressly requires both during shooting and provides a detailed reshoot procedure if protection is lost (3.2.5).
- **Start condition:** Viking normally starts with a loaded, holstered handgun and a loaded rifle at low ready (4.4). SRA uses a detailed command sequence and, in a multi-gun stage, normally keeps a non-starting pistol without a chambered round (4.2.1).
- **Warnings and commands:** SRA defines a complete command sequence, including `AVBRYT/STOP` and the preventive `MANOVER/WATCH OUT` warning (4.2). Viking does not codify an equivalent full command protocol.
- **Technical-malfunction exception:** SRA distinguishes a verified technical accidental discharge within the shooting limits and may award zero for the stage instead of an immediate first DQ (3.3.19). Viking provides no equivalent exception.

**Conclusion on Viking safety:** Viking is not merely similar at a general level. Many of its operative tests, exceptions, distances, and DQ triggers closely track the conventional SRA/practical-shooting model. The similarity is high, although Viking is less procedurally complete.

### Current Brutality compared with SRA

Current Brutality and SRA address many of the same hazards:

- Both state the four fundamental firearm-safety principles (Brutality 1.1; SRA 3.1).
- Both use a cold-range model and restrict handling to supervised or designated places (Brutality 1.2-1.4; SRA 3.3.1, 3.3.5-3.3.8).
- Both regulate transport, chamber flags, muzzle direction, trigger-finger position, dropped firearms, abandonment, protective equipment, unsafe ammunition, and accidental discharges.
- Their close-ground accidental-discharge limits are effectively the same: 10 ft in Brutality and 3 m in SRA.
- Their handgun steel limits are close: 10 yd (9.14 m) in Brutality and 8 m in SRA. Brutality's 50 yd rifle limit is much more restrictive than general SRA's 20 m, but close to SRA's 50 m Swedish military provision.

The shared topics conceal several major differences in safety philosophy:

| Issue | SRA/TTS | Current Brutality | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Status of an unloaded firearm during a stage | Muzzle sectors and unsafe-handling rules generally apply to the firearm used in the stage; the rules do not create a general "stick" exemption (3.3.10-3.3.14, 3.4). | RO-verified-clear and Condition 3 firearms are treated as sticks, exempt from sweeping, 180, and dropped-firearm DQs (1.5.1-1.5.2, 2.4.2.1-2.4.2.3). | **Fundamental divergence** |
| Condition 3 transitions | SRA uses unloaded, loaded, and carried-but-not-used concepts, with detailed RO commands. | Defines Condition 3 and different rules for manual and semi-automatic firearms, including in-stage transitions (1.5.2). | **Brutality-specific system** |
| Loaded holstering | Allowed under prescribed safety/hammer conditions; a stage may not require it (3.3.9). | Hot holstering is prohibited; during a stage it is an immediate DQ (1.6.4). | **Brutality is stricter** |
| Trigger-finger violation | Unsafe finger placement is a DQ rule (3.3.12-3.3.13). | DQ follows failure to correct after a `Finger!` warning (2.4.2.4). | **Brutality gives an express warning first** |
| Dropping/losing control | Unsafe handling can cause DQ; only an RO retrieves a dropped gun (3.3.4, 3.3.14). | Loaded firearm: DQ. Condition 3 or RO-clear firearm: +60 seconds only (2.4.2.1). | **Materially different consequence** |
| DQ scope | DQ is from the competition, subject to RM verification (3.2.3). | Normally applies only to the particular registered run and that shooting day (2.3). | **Brutality is narrower** |
| Staff carrying loaded guns | No comparable exception in SRA. | Non-competing match staff may carry loaded handguns, subject to facility rules (1.2). | **Brutality-specific exception** |
| Evidence and adjudication | RM verification; formal protest rules generally exclude safety findings, though film may be reviewed (3.2.3, 8.2). | MD confirmation, limited participants in the discussion, and video expressly encouraged (2.2). | **Different governance model** |

**Conclusion on current Brutality safety:** the two rulesets share a substantial practical-shooting safety vocabulary, but they are not close substitutes. The Condition 3/RO-clear exemptions, warning threshold, hot-holstering rule, and limited scope of a DQ produce materially different outcomes from the same incident.

## Focus area 2: overall breadth and closeness

### Coverage by subject

| Regulatory area | SRA/TTS | Viking Brutality | Current Brutality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose, eligibility, and competition levels | Detailed, including Swedish eligibility and organizational rules | Not covered | Not covered |
| Match officials and responsibilities | Detailed roles for MD, RM, RO, scorekeeper, supervisor, and secretary | MD/RM/Event Official mentioned, duties mostly undefined | MD/RO mentioned, duties mostly embedded in procedures |
| Stage design and construction | Detailed principles for balance, freedom of solution, barriers, fault lines, tunnels, and target distances | Mostly delegated to stage briefing | Mostly delegated to stage description |
| Range commands | Full Swedish/Finnish/English sequence and procedures | Not systematically defined | `STOP`, RO calls, and make-ready concepts, but no full sequence |
| Scoring | Four scoring methods, hit factor/stage points, target zones, penalties, normalization, classes, and approved result software | Time Plus; par time; generally +60-second penalties | Time Plus; par time; penalties and bonuses |
| Target rules | Detailed target construction, visibility, calibration, scoring overlays, pass-throughs, and disputed hits | Basic neutralization rules for paper, falling, reactive, frangible, and no-shoot targets | Basic neutralization rules for static, falling, spinner, paper, and self-healing targets |
| Divisions and equipment | Open, Standard, and Military framework; six appendices with firearm and carried-equipment specifications | Three named divisions, but detailed requirements are not present in the supplied rulebook | Division requirements delegated to separate CQB/Mainline documents |
| Reshoots | Detailed entitlements, mandatory reshoots, scoring effects, and exclusions | RM approval mentioned, without a complete test | Enumerated grounds plus one equipment-failure mulligan |
| Protests and result verification | Formal score signatures, correction periods, RM review, jury, deadlines, evidence, and fees | Final MD/RM authority; no full appeal process | MD resolves uncovered matters and confirms DQs; no general formal protest system |
| Insurance/liability | Not a main rulebook subject | Detailed participant insurance and release provisions | Not covered in the supplied rules |
| Shotgun-specific rules | Separate equipment appendix | No substantial shotgun framework | Detailed ammunition limits and equipment references |

### Viking's overall closeness to SRA

Viking is best described as a **selective, simplified event rulebook built around an SRA-like safety core**, not as a compact version of the whole SRA sport.

It is close to SRA in:

- the cold-range and safety-area operating model;
- the catalogue of DQ-level unsafe acts;
- firearm transport and condition requirements;
- firearm serviceability, prohibited ammunition, and holster restrictions;
- written stage briefings, target-neutralization concepts, reshoots, and official oversight.

It is not close to SRA in:

- **scoring:** Viking is Time Plus and lowest time wins; SRA primarily converts points and time into stage points, and highest match points wins;
- **institutional depth:** Viking does not reproduce SRA's official roles, match levels, eligibility, result verification, or jury process;
- **stage governance:** Viking leaves far more to stage briefings and official discretion;
- **technical depth:** Viking lacks SRA's extensive target-scoring, calibration, firearm-class, and equipment provisions;
- **competition identity:** Viking uses Irons/Open/Armored divisions and event-specific liability terms rather than SRA's broader sport and military framework.

Thus, Viking is **close in safety mechanics but wide apart in sporting architecture**.

### Current Brutality's overall closeness to SRA

Current Brutality is also much narrower than SRA, but it devotes unusual detail to multi-gun transitions and firearm states. Its four main sections cover safety/DQ, match procedure and Time Plus scoring, and general firearm/equipment rules. SRA covers these subjects plus the design and administration of the sport itself.

The largest overall differences are:

1. **Different scoring philosophy.** Brutality totals elapsed time, penalties, and bonuses; lowest wins (3.3). SRA supports several point-based methods and normally awards normalized stage points; highest total wins (6.5-6.7).
2. **Different hierarchy of authority.** Brutality expressly makes an RO ruling override the written stage description, which overrides division and general rules (3.4.2). SRA requires officials and stage descriptions to remain within the rule framework and provides review/protest routes (4.1.1, 8.2).
3. **Less standardized administration.** Brutality has no equivalent to SRA's detailed official qualifications, score verification, jury, protest deadlines, or competition-level rules.
4. **More event discretion.** Stage documents and separate division books carry much of the operative content. SRA places more of the sport's common standard in the central rulebook and appendices.
5. **Different reshoot policy.** Brutality can grant one equipment-failure mulligan in eligible divisions (3.9.6). SRA expressly denies a reshoot for a competitor's firearm malfunction (8.1.5).
6. **Different equipment emphasis.** Brutality requires a stable match-long equipment configuration and separately regulates accessories and shotgun loads. SRA gives detailed firearm-division tables and a prescriptive military equipment list, while allowing more equipment changes with authorization where required.

Current Brutality is therefore **recognizably from the same broad family of dynamic shooting rules, but not especially close to SRA as a complete regulatory system**.

## Final assessment

### How similar is Viking Brutality to the Finnish/SRA regulations?

**Safety: highly similar.** Viking follows an SRA-like conventional safety regime and often reaches the same result through nearly identical criteria. The 3 m/10 ft accidental-discharge boundary, safety-area ammunition ban, 180-degree rule, trigger-finger rules, official retrieval of dropped guns, and 8 m handgun steel distance are especially close indicators.

**Overall: moderately similar, but substantially narrower.** Viking retains the practical event layer while omitting much of SRA's institutional, technical, scoring, and appeal structure. Its Time Plus system is a major departure.

### How close are the current Brutality regulations to SRA?

**Safety: moderately similar in topics, less close in operative effect.** The shared cold-range and DQ concepts are significant, but Brutality's firearm-state exemptions and sanction rules are fundamental departures.

**Overall: low to moderate similarity and much narrower scope.** Both regulate dynamic shooting events, but current Brutality is an event framework centered on Time Plus scoring and stage/division supplements. SRA is a comprehensive sport rulebook governing organization, stage construction, commands, technical scoring, divisions, records, reshoots, and protests.

In short: **Viking is closer to SRA on safety than current Brutality is; neither is close to SRA in total breadth.** Viking resembles a simplified SRA-style practical-shooting safety code combined with Brutality-style event scoring, while current Brutality has developed a distinct multi-gun safety and competition model.
