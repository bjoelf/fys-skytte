​1.1.​ ​Follow the four rules of firearms safety at all times:​
​1.1.1.​ ​Rule 1: Know and be able to justify the condition of your firearms at all times.​
​1.1.2.​ ​Rule 2: Keep your firearm pointed in a safe direction.​
​1.1.3.​ ​Rule 3: Keep your finger out of the trigger guard when not actively engaging targets.​
​1.1.4.​ ​Rule 4: Know your target, its background, and its foreground before engagement.​
​1.2.​ ​Brutality​​Matches​​are​​run​​on​​Cold​​Ranges.​​Unless​​under​​direct​​RO​​supervision​​and​​given​
​the​ ​command​ ​to​ ​preload​ ​or​ ​load,​ ​every​ ​firearm​ ​must​ ​remain​ ​completely​ ​free​ ​of​ ​live​
​ammunition​ ​or​ ​anything​ ​that​ ​may​ ​be​ ​mistaken​ ​by​ ​an​ ​RO​ ​for​ ​live​ ​ammunition​​(e.g.​​casings,​
​snap​ ​caps,​ ​etc.).​ ​Subject​ ​to​ ​host​ ​facility​​restrictions,​​match​​staff​​are​​permitted​​to​​conceal​​or​
​open carry loaded handguns on days they are not themselves competing.​
​1.3.​ ​Chamber​​flags​​are​​required​​to​​be​​used​​when​​transporting​​any​​firearm​​outside​​of​​a​​holster​​or​
​case.​ ​A​ ​chamber​ ​flag​​must​​both​​hold​​the​​action​​open​​AND​​be​​clearly​​visible.​​A​​spent​​metal​
​casing may not be used as a chamber flag.​
​1.3.1.​ ​While​ ​under​ ​RO​ ​supervision,​ ​chamber​ ​flags​ ​may​ ​be​ ​temporarily​ ​removed​ ​in​ ​order​ ​to​
​facilitate pictures and/or video.​
​1.4.​ ​Handling of firearms on the range will only occur in one of the following conditions:​
​1.4.1.​ ​While under the direct supervision of an RO.​
​1.4.2.​ ​At a designated preloading or safety area, muzzle pointed directly into a berm.​
​1.4.3.​ ​Being safely carried to, from, and between stages:​
​1.4.3.1.​ ​Long​ ​guns​ ​flagged​ ​or​ ​with​ ​manual​ ​actions​ ​open​ ​and​ ​carried​ ​or​ ​slung​ ​muzzle​ ​up​ ​or​
​down​​without​​sweeping​​anyone.​​The​​use​​of​​bags,​​cases​​or​​carts​​are​​permitted​​unless​
​otherwise specified.​
​1.4.3.2.​ ​Handguns​​securely​​contained​​in​​a​​worn​​holster.​​Detachable​​holsters​​may​​be​​removed​
​from​ ​or​ ​emplaced​ ​on​ ​a​ ​belt​ ​at​​a​​designated​​preloading​​or​​safety​​area​​so​​long​​as​​the​

​1.5.1.​ ​RO-verified​ ​clear​ ​firearms​ ​are​ ​completely​ ​devoid​ ​of​ ​ammunition​ ​and​ ​detachable​
​magazines.​ ​RO-verified​ ​clear​ ​firearms​ ​are​ ​treated​ ​as​ ​a​ ​stick​ ​not​ ​subject​ ​to​ ​sweeping​
​violations,​ ​180​ ​violations,​ ​or​ ​a​ ​DQ​ ​if​ ​control​ ​is​ ​lost.​ ​A​ ​competitor​​and​​RO​​can​​set​​up​​an​
​RO-verified clear firearm through one of the following procedures:​
​1.5.1.1.​ ​While making ready, the competitor pointedly does NOT load the firearm.​
​1.5.1.2.​ ​While making ready, the competitor shows clear for the RO.​
​1.5.1.3.​ ​During​ ​the​ ​stage,​ ​the​ ​competitor​ ​drops​ ​their​ ​detachable​ ​magazine​ ​and​ ​performs​ ​a​
​clearing shot on a target.​
​1.5.1.4.​ ​During​ ​the​ ​stage,​ ​the​ ​competitor​ ​unloads​ ​and​ ​shows​ ​clear​ ​OR​ ​shoots​ ​their​ ​gun​ ​to​
​empty and shows clear. Ideally, this will be communicated to the RO in advance.​
​1.5.1.5.​ ​It​ ​is​ ​the​ ​competitor's​ ​responsibility​ ​to​ ​ensure​ ​the​​RO​​is​​able​​to​​clearly​​see​​the​​empty​
​magazine / magazine-well and empty chamber.​
​1.5.1.6.​ ​The​ ​firearm​ ​is​ ​not​ ​considered​ ​RO-verified​ ​clear​ ​until​ ​the​ ​RO​ ​has​ ​verbally​
​confirmed that it is clear.​
​1.5.2.​ ​Condition​​3​​firearms​​have​​their​​action​​closed,​​their​​hammer​​or​​striker​​down​​on​​an​​empty​
​chamber​ ​when​ ​possible,​ ​with​ ​a​ ​detachable​ ​magazine​ ​in​ ​the​ ​gun​ ​or​ ​ammunition​ ​in​ ​an​
​integral​​magazine.​​Until​​loaded,​​Condition​​3​​firearms​​are​​treated​​as​​a​​stick​​not​​subject​​to​
​sweeping violations, 180 violations, or a DQ if control is lost.​
​1.5.2.1.​ ​Detachable​ ​magazines​ ​and/or​ ​ammunition​ ​on​ ​external​ ​holders​ ​that​ ​cannot​ ​directly​
​feed​ ​ammunition​ ​into​ ​the​ ​chamber​ ​(such​ ​as​ ​side​ ​saddles,​ ​match-savers,​ ​or​
​stock/foregrip-mounted mag holders) do not make a weapon Condition 3.​
​1.5.2.2.​ ​A​ ​firearm​​may​​be​​brought​​to​​Condition​​3​​while​​making​​ready​​or​​preloading​​under​​RO​
​supervision.​​The​​competitor​​must​​show​​clear,​​close​​the​​action,​​pull​​the​​trigger​​if​​doing​
​so​ ​does​ ​not​ ​prevent​ ​loading​ ​of​ ​a​ ​magazine,​​load​​a​​magazine​​under​​RO​​supervision,​
​and pointedly NOT chamber a round.​
​1.5.2.3.​ ​Manual​ ​firearms​ ​may​ ​be​ ​made​ ​Condition​ ​3​ ​during​ ​a​ ​stage​ ​by​ ​firing​ ​a​ ​shot​ ​and​
​pointedly NOT cycling the action, leaving the hammer or striker down on a fired case.​
​1.5.2.4.​ ​Semi-automatic​ ​firearms​ ​may​ ​start​ ​a​ ​stage​ ​Condition​ ​3​ ​but​ ​CANNOT​ ​be​ ​made​
​Condition​ ​3​ ​or​ ​returned​ ​to​ ​Condition​ ​3​ ​during​ ​a​ ​stage.​ ​Once​ ​a​ ​stage​ ​has​ ​begun,​
​semi-auto firearms may only become Loaded or RO-verified clear.​
​1.5.3.​ ​Loaded​ ​firearms​ ​either​ ​have​ ​ammunition​​chambered​​or​​(as​​in​​the​​case​​of​​firearms​​that​
​are​ ​being​ ​reloaded​ ​or​ ​remediated)​ ​are​ ​to​ ​be​ ​treated​ ​as​ ​though​ ​they​ ​have​ ​ammunition​
​chambered for the sake of safety.​
​1.6.​ ​Firearm​ ​transitions.​ ​A​ ​competitor​ ​may​ ​not​ ​simultaneously​ ​handle​ ​two​ ​loaded​ ​firearms​ ​as​
​defined above. In order to transition away from a firearm by slinging or holstering:​
​1.6.1.​ ​Manual firearms must be made either RO-verified clear or Condition 3 as provided above.​
​1.6.2.​ ​Semi-automatic firearms must be made RO-verified clear as provided above.​
​1.6.3.​ ​Where​ ​a​ ​dump​​box​​or​​table​​or​​other​​abandonment​​location​​is​​provided,​​firearms​​may​​be​
​grounded in one of the following states:​
​1.6.3.1.​ ​With all safety mechanisms activated, muzzle pointed in a designated safe direction.​
​1.6.3.2.​ ​Condition 3.​
​1.5.​​ .6.3.3.​ ​RO-verified clear.​

​1.7.​ ​Eye & Ear Protection​
​1.7.1.​ ​Eye​​protection​​must​​be​​worn​​by​​every​​individual​​(even​​non-competitors)​​at​​all​​times​​while​
​on the range.​
​1.7.2.​ ​Ear​​protection​​must​​be​​worn​​by​​every​​individual​​(even​​non-competitors)​​at​​all​​times​​while​
​on or near a stage.​
​1.8.​ ​Minimum target engagement distances:​
​1.8.1.​ ​For​​rifle​​calibers​​and​​shotgun​​slugs,​​no​​steel​​target​​shall​​be​​engaged​​at​​distances​​shorter​
​than 50 yards.​
​1.8.2.​ ​For​ ​pistol​​calibers,​​buckshot,​​and​​birdshot,​​no​​steel​​target​​shall​​be​​engaged​​at​​distances​
​shorter than 10 yards.​
​2.​
​Disqualifications​
​2.1.​
I​f​ ​any​ ​RO​ ​or​ ​competitor​ ​witnesses​ ​a​ ​potential​ ​safety​ ​issue​ ​or​​disqualifying​​violation,​
​they​ ​are​ ​to​ ​immediately​ ​yell​ ​the​​"STOP!"​​command.​​If​​called​​by​​a​​competitor,​​they​​must​
​explain to the RO what they witnessed.​
​2.2.​ ​All​ ​potential​ ​disqualifications​ ​must​ ​be​​escalated​​to​​the​​Match​​Director​​for​​confirmation​
​with the RO(s) and the involved competitor.​
​2.2.1.​ ​Confirmation​ ​discussions​ ​will​ ​be​ ​confined​ ​to​ ​the​ ​competitor,​ ​the​ ​RO(s),​ ​and​ ​the​ ​Match​
​Director.​ ​If​ ​other​ ​competitors​ ​witnessed​ ​the​ ​potential​ ​violation,​ ​the​ ​Match​ ​Director​ ​may​
​interview them.​
​2.2.2.​ ​Video​​evidence​​is​​encouraged​​and​​welcomed​​in​​order​​to​​assist​​with​​confirming​​whether​
​a disqualifying violation has occurred.​
​2.3.​ ​If​ ​the​ ​competitor​ ​has​ ​registered​ ​multiple​ ​runs​ ​in​ ​the​ ​same​ ​Brutality,​ ​a​ ​confirmed​
​disqualification​​applies​​to​​the​​specific​​run​​in​​question​​and​​the​​competitor​​will​​not​​be​​permitted​
​to shoot further that day.​
​2.3.1.​ ​Additional​ ​runs​ ​on​ ​prior​ ​or​ ​subsequent​ ​days​ ​are​ ​not​ ​affected​ ​unless​ ​the​ ​Match​ ​Director​
​determines the disqualifying violation to be particularly egregious.​
​2.3.2.​ ​The​ ​Match​ ​Director​ ​may​ ​choose​ ​to​ ​allow​ ​the​ ​competitor​ ​to​ ​move​ ​additional​​runs​​on​​the​
​same day to other shooting days to permit them to complete those entries.​
​2.4.​ ​In​​addition​​to​​violations​​of​​Range​​Safety​​rules​​as​​described​​in​​Section​​1,​​the​​following​
​violations are subject to immediate disqualification and/or removal from the match:​
​2.4.1.​ ​Accidental or negligent discharges​​, including:​
​2.4.1.1.​ ​Any shot that is clearly not aimed at a valid target.​
​2.4.1.2.​ ​A​​shot​​which​​directly​​travels​​over​​a​​backstop​​/​​berm​​or​​in​​any​​other​​direction​​specified​
​as​​unsafe,​​except​​that​​a​​shot​​legitimately​​fired​​at​​a​​target​​which​​then​​changes​​direction​
​and travels in an unsafe direction is NOT a disqualifying violation.​
​2.4.1.3.​ ​A​ ​shot​ ​which​ ​strikes​​the​​ground​​within​​10​​feet​​of​​the​​participant​​(or​​would​​have​​if​​not​
​deflected or stopped by a prop), except when shooting at a target closer than 10 feet.​​ .4.1.4.​ ​A shot that occurs while preloading, making ready or unloading a firearm.​
2
​2.4.1.5.​ ​A shot that occurs while reloading or fixing a malfunction.​
​2.4.1.6.​ ​A shot that occurs while picking up or setting down a firearm​
​2.4.1.7.​ ​A shot that occurs while changing hands or shoulders.​
​2.4.1.8.​ ​A shot that occurs while moving, unless intentionally shooting at targets on the move.​
​2.4.2.​ ​Unsafe​ ​gun​ ​handling​​,​ ​including​ ​the​ ​following​ ​examples​ ​and​ ​any​​other​​actions​​deemed​
​unsafe by the Match Director:​
​2.4.2.1.​ ​Losing​ ​control​ ​of​ ​a​​Loaded​​firearm​​-​​if​​this​​occurs,​​the​​RO​​must​​retrieve​​and​​clear​
​the​​firearm​​(losing​​control​​of​​a​​Condition​​3​​or​​RO-verified​​clear​​firearm​​is​​not​​subject​​to​
​disqualification, but incurs a +60 second penalty).​
​2.4.2.2.​ ​Allowing​ ​the​ ​muzzle​ ​of​ ​a​ ​Loaded​ ​firearm​ ​to​ ​point​ ​uprange​ ​of​ ​the​ ​180​ ​or​ ​other​
​designated muzzle-safe boundary (not applied to Condition 3 or RO-verified clear).​
​2.4.2.3.​ ​Allowing​ ​the​​muzzle​​of​​a​​Loaded​​firearm​​to​​point​​at​​any​​part​​of​​the​​competitor's​​body​
​during a course of fire (not applied to Condition 3 or RO-verified clear).​
​2.4.2.4.​ ​Failing​​to​​keep​​the​​finger​​outside​​the​​trigger​​guard​​while​​not​​shooting​​after​​a​​"Finger!"​
​warning from the RO (except when carefully de-cocking a firearm).​
​2.4.3.​ ​Unsafe,​ ​unsportsmanlike,​ ​or​ ​rude​ ​conduct​​,​ ​including​​the​​following​​examples​​and​​any​
​other actions deemed unsafe or uncouth by the Match Director:​
​2.4.3.1.​ ​Being​​intoxicated​​or​​under​​the​​influence​​of​​mentally​​or​​physically​​impairing​​substances​
​while competing.​
​2.4.3.2.​ ​Blatant​ ​unsportsmanlike​​conduct,​​attempts​​to​​cheat,​​or​​aggressive​​behavior​​(physical​
​or verbal) towards any person.​
​2.4.3.3.​ ​Refusal to contribute towards stage reset.​
​2.4.3.4.​ ​Actions or displays intended to disrupt or offend other competitors and/or match staff.​

​Match Procedures & Scoring​
​ ​​Zeroing​​Bay​​may​​be​​designated​​by​​match​​staff​​to​​be​​made​​available​​for​​zero​​confirmation​
A
​and​ ​function-checking​ ​of​ ​firearms.​ ​Actions​ ​within​ ​a​ ​zeroing​ ​bay​ ​must​ ​be​ ​supervised​ ​by​ ​an​
​RO.​​If an RO is not present, the zeroing bay is closed.​
​3.2.​ ​Competitors​ ​must​ ​be​ ​prepared​ ​to​ ​shoot​ ​in​ ​any​ ​conditions;​ ​stages​ ​will​ ​continue​ ​during​
​appropriate​ ​light​ ​levels​ ​and​ ​will​ ​not​ ​delay​ ​for​ ​environmental​ ​considerations​ ​except​ ​due​ ​to​
​unsafe circumstances such as lightning or severe weather.​
​3.3.​ ​Brutality​ ​Matches​ ​use​ ​Time​ ​Plus​ ​scoring​​;​ ​a​ ​competitor's​ ​time​ ​on​ ​each​ ​stage​ ​plus​ ​any​
​penalties​ ​or​ ​bonuses​ ​determines​ ​their​​score,​​and​​a​​competitor's​​stage​​scores​​are​​totaled​​to​
​produce their match score; the competitor with the lowest match score wins.​
​3.4.​ ​Each stage will have a written description, including its par time, bonuses, and penalties.​
​3.4.1.​ ​The​​written​​stage​​description​​will​​be​​read​​to​​competitors​​as​​part​​of​​a​​stage​​brief​​along​​with​
​any​​clarifications​​deemed​​necessary​​by​​the​​RO.​​Q&A​​time​​will​​be​​held​​after​​the​​reading​​of​
​the written stage description.​
​3.4.2.​ ​If​ ​conflicting​ ​instructions​ ​arise,​ ​RO​​rulings​​overrule​​the​​written​​stage​​description​​,​​the​
​stage​​description​​overrules​​the​​division​​rulebook,​​and​​the​​division​​rulebook​​overrules​​the​
​general match rules.​
​3.5.​ ​Unless otherwise stated by the stage brief, competitors will begin each stage as follows:​
​3.1.​​ .5.1.​ ​Standing at the start location, securely wearing all equipment.​
3
​3.5.2.​ ​First firearm Loaded held at low ready, all other firearms holstered or slung, Condition 3.​
​3.6.​ ​Competitors must complete the entirety of each stage within its par time.​
​3.6.1.​ ​Failure​​to​​complete​​the​​stage​​within​​par​​or​​intentionally​​quitting​​a​​stage​​before​​completing​
​the​ ​course​ ​of​ ​fire​ ​will​ ​result​ ​in​ ​the​ ​competitor​ ​receiving​ ​a​ ​score​ ​of​ ​the​ ​par​ ​time​ ​plus​
​penalties for any missed targets and incomplete stage elements.​
​3.6.2.​ ​Competitors​​are​​permitted​​to​​skip​​a​​required​​target​​(by​​informing​​the​​RO​​they​​are​​skipping​
​the​​target)​​and​​accept​​the​​missed​​target​​penalty​​in​​order​​to​​complete​​the​​rest​​of​​the​​stage.​
​Competitors cannot make up skipped targets after leaving that shooting position.​
​3.7.​ ​Targets must be neutralized as follows:​
​3.7.1.​ ​Static​​targets​​must​​be​​hit​​a​​required​​number​​of​​times.​​The​​RO​​will​​call​​"Hit,"​​"Impact,"​​or​
​the number of hits observed to confirm neutralization.​
​3.7.2.​ ​Falling​ ​targets​ ​must​​fall​​or​​otherwise​​move​​to​​the​​RO's​​satisfaction.​​If​​the​​RO​​observes​
​three solid hits to the calibration zone, they will call that target neutralized.​
​3.7.3.​ ​Spinners​ ​must​ ​be​ ​hit​ ​a​ ​required​ ​number​ ​of​ ​times​ ​OR​ ​must​ ​be​ ​completely​ ​rotated.​​The​
​RO will call the number of hits observed or "Over," to confirm neutralization.​
​3.7.4.​ ​Paper or self-healing targets​​will be scored per stage​​instructions.​
​3.7.5.​ ​If​ ​an​ ​RO​ ​mistakenly​ ​calls​ ​a​ ​target​ ​neutralized,​ ​the​ ​competitor​ ​may​ ​choose​ ​to​ ​treat​ ​that​
​target as neutralized and move on.​
​3.8.​ ​ROs​​will​​coach​​all​​competitors​​during​​the​​course​​of​​fire.​​If​​a​​competitor​​does​​not​​wish​​to​​be​
​coached, it is their responsibility to notify the RO during make ready.​
​3.9.​ ​Reshoots​​will be granted (and the Match Director notified)​​for the following circumstances:​
​3.9.1.​ ​RO error or interference.​
​3.9.2.​ ​Safety concerns outside of the shooter's control.​
​3.9.3.​ ​Prop or target failure beyond the ability to improvise a roughly-equivalent alternative.​
​3.9.4.​ ​Timer failure that cannot be substituted with video evidence.​
​3.9.5.​ ​Any other stage-ending failure outside the shooter's control or responsibility.​
​3.9.6.​ ​Competitors​ ​may​ ​request​​a​​mulligan​​(reshoot​​for​​competitor's​​equipment​​failure)​​if​​they​
​suffer a stage-ending malfunction prior to the final shooting position on a stage.​
​3.9.6.1.​ ​The Match Director must approve all mulligans.​
​3.9.6.2.​ ​Competitors may only use one mulligan in a division.​
​3.9.6.3.​ ​Some divisions prohibit the use of mulligans.​

​4.4.2.​ ​Pistol​ ​ammunition​ ​must​ ​be​ ​minimum​ ​.32ACP,​ ​maximum​ ​10mm​ ​or​ ​.45LC,​ ​with​ ​a​
​maximum​ ​velocity​ ​of​ ​1600FPS​ ​(5.7mm​ ​polymer-tipped​ ​projectiles​ ​specifically​ ​are​
​permitted to exceed this maximum), with 9x19mm Parabellum as the expected standard.​
​4.4.3.​ ​Rifle​ ​ammunition​ ​must​ ​be​ ​minimum​ ​.30​ ​carbine,​ ​maximum​ ​of​ ​a​ ​traditional​ ​battle​ ​rifle​
​caliber (.308, 8mm Mauser, etc.), with 5.56x45mm NATO as the expected standard.​
​4.4.4.​ ​Shotgun loads​​must be lead only, and​​may be further​​governed by the host facility​​.​
​4.4.4.1.​ ​Shells must be minimum 20-gauge and 2.75 inch or larger.​
​4.4.4.2.​ ​Birdshot must be #6 shot or smaller (#7, #7.5 #8, etc.).​
​4.4.4.3.​ ​All shell types are limited to a maximum velocity of 1600FPS.​
