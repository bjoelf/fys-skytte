# Fys-skytte

An open research project exploring rules for Swedish **two-gun physical action shooting**: competitions that combine practical rifle and handgun shooting with movement, physical tasks, and problem-solving.

The repository currently collects and compares rules and regulations for 2G-ACM, Brutality, Viking Brutality, SRA/TTS, and IPSC. The analysis focuses primarily on:

- firearm and range safety;
- stage design and competitor information;
- scoring, penalties, and match administration;
- similarities and material differences between the rule systems.

The long-term goal is to develop an open-source Swedish regulation that any club or organizer can use to run safe, fair, and repeatable two-gun physical action shooting competitions.

## Project status

This is an early research and drafting project. The collected documents and comparison reports are working material, not an approved competition rulebook. They may contain interpretation errors or become outdated as source rules change.

Nothing in this repository replaces applicable Swedish law, range instructions, permit conditions, federation rules, insurance requirements, or the directions of qualified match and range officials.

## Repository contents

- [`source documents/`](source%20documents/) contains text conversions of the source rulebooks used for analysis and a list of their original locations.
- [`comparison reports/rules comparison.md`](comparison%20reports/rules%20comparison.md) compares the safety models and overall regulatory breadth of SRA/TTS, Viking Brutality, and Brutality.
- [`comparison reports/stage-design-comparison.md`](comparison%20reports/stage-design-comparison.md) compares stage-design principles in SRA/TTS, Brutality, and IPSC.

## Research principles

- Prefer current primary-source rulebooks over summaries or customary practice.
- Distinguish documented rules from observations, assumptions, and local event practice.
- Compare practical effects, not only similar wording.
- Record the version or publication date of each source where available.
- Treat safety, competitive fairness, accessibility, and ease of administration as separate design concerns.

## Organisations, regulations, and background

### Brutality
Today’s Brutality began in the U.S. in 2009 as “2G-ACM” 
https://www.brutalitymatches.org/about-brutality

Brutality is a registered trademark in the U.S.
https://www.trademarkia.com/brutality-97216085

Brutality is also a registered trademark in Finland owned by Varusteleka Oy Finland. It is likely licensed from the U.S., but this has not yet been confirmed.

https://varusteleka.com/en-se/pages/finnish-brutality-history

### 2G-ACM
Brutality is a derivative of “2G-ACM” (Two Gun Action Challenge Match)
2G-ACM, on the other hand, is an open concept that is free to use.
https://www.brutalitymatches.org/brutality-trademark

There are published derivatives of 2G-ACM (likely not a complete list):
https://www.fxrgc.org/practical-shooting/2g-acm/
https://www.cactusmatch.com/rules

### Viking Brutality
Swedish Viking Brutality is organized by a privately owned company. They have their own rulebook. It is likely that the name “Brutality” is licensed by the organizer, but this has not yet been confirmed.
https://www.vikingbrutality.com/

### SRA
SRA was developed for Finnish reservists. In Sweden, SRA is only available to those with a connection to the Swedish Armed Forces (FM).

In Sweden, SRA has been organized by the Swedish Pistol Shooting Federation since 2023 at the request of the Swedish Armed Forces (FM).
https://www.pistolskytteforbundet.se/om-pistolskytte/sra/

SRA is a trademark owned by RESUL (the Finnish Reservist Organization)
https://resul.fi/uutinen/sra-tavaramerkki-myonnetty-resulille/
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SRA-shooting

International Federation of SRA, IFSRA
https://ifsra.wordpress.com/about

## Intended further work

The next phase is to turn the research into a Swedish rulebook that is practical for independent organizers. Expected work includes:

1. Define the purpose, principles, terminology, and scope of the discipline.
2. Establish a complete safety model, including range condition, firearm handling, commands, abandonment, disqualifications, protective equipment, and target distances.
3. Define stage-design requirements for shooting challenges, physical tasks, briefings, walkthroughs, consistency, and accessibility.
4. Select transparent scoring and penalty rules that balance speed, accuracy, and physical performance.
5. Specify divisions, equipment rules, official roles, reshoots, protests, and result handling.
6. Test draft rules at small matches and revise them from documented organizer, official, and competitor feedback.
7. Publish versioned releases in Swedish, with an English translation where useful.

## Contributing

Contributions are welcome, particularly from match organizers, range officers, competitors, shooting clubs, and people with relevant Swedish legal or federation experience.

Useful contributions include:

- corrections supported by a primary source and a precise rule reference;
- newer editions of source documents;
- additional rulesets relevant to two-gun physical action shooting;
- structured review of the comparison reports;
- proposals for safety, stage design, scoring, or match-administration rules;
- reports from practical testing of future drafts.

When proposing a change, clearly separate verifiable facts about an existing ruleset from recommendations for the future Swedish rules. Do not include personal data or documents that cannot lawfully be redistributed.
